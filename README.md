# Aquatic Insect Biodiversity Monitoring Using Environmental DNA (eDNA)

### Master's Thesis Research | RomaNatura Protected Areas, Rome, Italy

## Research Overview

This repository presents my Master's thesis research in Biodiversity and Environmental Conservation, conducted within the ROMAeDNA research project.

The study investigated aquatic insect biodiversity across the RomaNatura protected area system in Rome, Italy, using environmental DNA (eDNA) metabarcoding.

The research aimed to assess how aquatic habitat characteristics and urbanisation patterns are associated with taxonomic richness and community composition.

## Research Objectives

- Characterise aquatic insect diversity across different freshwater habitats.
- Compare taxonomic richness and community composition between ditches and ponds.
- Investigate potential relationships between biodiversity and the urbanisation gradient.
- Explore the potential of eDNA metabarcoding for urban freshwater biodiversity monitoring.

## Methodology

The study involved 34 freshwater sampling sites distributed across 11 protected areas within the RomaNatura system.

Environmental DNA was collected from water samples and analysed using metabarcoding techniques targeting mitochondrial COI and 16S markers.

The ecological assessment considered aquatic insect richness, community composition, habitat differences and spatial patterns.

## Key Findings

- A total of 183 insect taxa were detected across the sampled sites.
- Ditches showed higher expected taxonomic richness than ponds, with particularly marked differences for EPT taxa, Odonata and Hemiptera.
- Habitat type was significantly associated with differences in community composition.
- Distance from Rome's city centre, used as a proxy for urbanisation, was not a robust predictor of aquatic insect richness.

These findings highlight the importance of freshwater habitat characteristics and ecological connectivity for biodiversity conservation in urban protected areas.

## Research Significance

This research contributes to the understanding of aquatic insect biodiversity in urban freshwater ecosystems and demonstrates the potential of eDNA metabarcoding as a complementary tool for ecological monitoring and conservation.
